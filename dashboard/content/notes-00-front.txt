@grp The 90-second protocol for every MCQ
- **10 s** – name the trap class: support, convention, hypothesis of a theorem, sign, invariance.
- **40 s** – apply the stored result; compute only what the options differ in.
- **20 s** – kill options with a special value ($n=1$, $\rho=0$, $p=\tfrac12$, $x=0$) or a range check.
- **Decide** – answer whenever one option is dead; leave only true blanks.
@grp Option-killers that work on any stem
-* **Range:** probabilities in $[0,1]$; $|r|\le1$; $R_{1.23}\ge|r_{12}|$; $\eta\ge|r|$; variances $\ge0$; a CDF ends at 1.
-* **Invariance:** $r$, $\eta^2$, $Q$ and $C$ ignore change of origin and scale; only the sign of $r$ can flip.
-* **Convention:** geometric and negative binomial (trials or failures), exponential (rate or mean), gamma (rate or scale).
-* **Support first:** a non-rectangular support means dependence and fixes every integration limit.
-* **Limits:** $n\to\infty$, $\rho\to0$ or $\theta\to0$ must reproduce the known special case.

@grp The statements
| Looks right | Correct version | Why |
| 5 draws without replacement from 20 items, 8 marked: variance of the marked count $=9/19$ | **18/19** | $n\tfrac KN\left(1-\tfrac KN\right)\tfrac{N-n}{N-1}=5(0.4)(0.6)\tfrac{15}{19}$ |
| $X\sim U(0,12)$: $P(5<X<7\mid X>6)=0$ | **1/6** ([2026 Q16]) | Intersect first: $\tfrac{P(6<X<7)}{P(X>6)}=\tfrac{1/12}{1/2}$ |
| Birthday: $P(\text{shared})=1-\sum(1-i/365)$ | **$1-\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1-i/365)$** | Successive “new birthday” events multiply |
| $R_{1.23}\ge r_{12},r_{13},r_{23}$ | $R_{1.23}\ge|r_{12}|,|r_{13}|,|r_{12.3}|,|r_{13.2}|$ only | $X_2=X_3$ with $X_1$ independent: $R=0$ but $r_{23}=1$ |
| $P\left(X=(-1)^{k-1}k\right)=\tfrac{6}{\pi^2k^2}$ obeys the WLLN by Khinchin | **No mean; the WLLN fails** | $\mathbb{E}\lvert X\rvert=\tfrac6{\pi^2}\sum\tfrac1k=\infty$ and $nP(\lvert X\rvert>n)\to\tfrac6{\pi^2}\ne0$ |
| $\mathbb{E}(S_m/S_n)=m/n$ for independent $X_k$ with a common mean | Needs **iid positive** $X_k$ | Symmetry then gives $\mathbb{E}(X_i/S_n)=1/n$ |
| Regression lines are perpendicular when $b_{yx}=-b_{xy}$ | **Perpendicular iff $r=0$** | The two $b$’s share the sign of $r$ |
| Inflexion points always sum to twice the mode | Only when symmetric about the mode (normal, $\chi^2$, $F$) | Not a general law |
| $\sigma_A=K\sigma_B$ makes A’s peak $1/K$ of B’s | True for normal curves **with equal totals** | Peak ordinate $=N/(\sigma\sqrt{2\pi})$ |
| $G=M\left(1-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2M^2}\right)$, $M^2-G^2=\sigma^2$, $H=M\left(1-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{M^2}\right)$ exactly | **Approximations** | Second-order expansions, valid for small $\sigma/M$ |
| Percentile kurtosis above 0.263 means leptokurtic | **Reversed**: below 0.263 lepto, above platy | Laplace 0.215, normal 0.263, uniform 0.3125 |
| A variable can have an MGF yet no moments | Only if the MGF is finite on one side of 0 | Finite on an open interval around 0 forces every moment |
| A consistent test is UMP | UMP $\Rightarrow$ unbiased; unbiased or consistent $\not\Rightarrow$ UMP | Consistency only says power $\to1$ |
@grp PYQ misprints to recognise in the hall
-! [2019 Q32] hypergeometric variance: option (b) prints $(m+n+1)$; the true denominator is $(m+n)^2(m+n-1)$. Still mark **(b)**.
-! [2022 Q12] no option equals $\mathbb{E}(T^2\bar X^2)=24/5=4.8$ (the correct key of [2019 Q25]); $24.2$ would be $\mathbb{E}(T^2)+\mathbb{E}(\bar X^2)$.
-! [2026 Q10] both $3/4$ and $3/5$ are offered: the trials convention gives $3/4$, the failures convention of Indian texts gives $3/5$.
-! [2019 Q18] statement 1 prints $a^2/(a-b)^2$; the quantity that must be an integer is $n=a^2/(a-b)$.
-! [2018 Q26] the printed joint pdf does not integrate to 1, but $Y\mid X=x\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\text{rate }1+x)$ still holds, so $\mathbb{E}(Y\mid x)=1/(1+x)$, option **(a)**.
