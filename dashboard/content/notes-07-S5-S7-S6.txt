@sec S7 | Regression and simple correlation | Statistical Methods
@meta codes=S7 rel=S8 pyq=8 f27=0.9 tier=3
@lede The two-regression-lines problem dominates: test which line is which with $b_{yx}b_{xy}\le1$, take $r=\pm\sqrt{b_{yx}b_{xy}}$ with their common sign, and remember both lines pass through $(\bar x,\bar y)$.
@grp Simple correlation r
$$r=\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y}=\frac{n\sum XY-\sum X\sum Y}{\sqrt{\left[n\sum X^2-(\sum X)^2\right]\left[n\sum Y^2-(\sum Y)^2\right]}},\qquad r=\frac{\sigma_{X+Y}^2-\sigma_{X-Y}^2}{4\sigma_X\sigma_Y}$$
-* $-1\le r\le1$, with $\pm1$ iff an exact line. $aX+bY+c=0$ exactly: $r=+1$ if $a,b$ have opposite signs, $-1$ if the same sign.
-* $r(a+bX,\,c+dY)=\mathrm{sign}(bd)\,r$. [2018 Q28] adding 3 leaves **0.65**; [2019 Q19] $Z=aY+b$ gives $\tfrac{a}{\lvert a\rvert}r$; [2026 Q25] $r(2x,y)=0.25$ gives $r\left(4x-3,\tfrac{7-4y}{8}\right)=$ **−0.25**.
- [2020 Q13] $\sigma_{X-Y}^2=\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2$ forces $r=$ **0**.
- [2019 Q10] $4.8=0.6\times3\times\sigma_Y$ gives $\sigma_Y=$ **2.67**; [2023 Q25] $32=0.8\times2\times\sigma_Y$ gives $V(Y)=$ **400**.
-! $r$ sees only linear association: [2022 Q11] $Y=(X-3)^2$ on $X=1,\dots,5$ gives $r=$ **0**; [2019 Q9] $Y=X-1$ gives $r=1$ while $r(X^2,Y^2)<0$, **(a)**.
- [2020 Q14] $Y=20-X$ makes $(X+Y)X=20X$, so $r=$ **1**.
- $r^2$ = share of variance explained; $1-r^2$ non-determination; $\sqrt{1-r^2}$ alienation. [2020 Q15] $r=0.7$: 49% explained, both $b$’s positive, **1 and 2**. The correlation of $y$ with fitted $\hat y$ is $\lvert r\rvert$.
- $\mathrm{SE}(r)=(1-r^2)/\sqrt n$; $\mathrm{PE}=0.6745\,\mathrm{SE}$; $r$ is significant if $r>6\,\mathrm{PE}$ and not if $r<\mathrm{PE}$.
- Rotation $U=X\cos\alpha+Y\sin\alpha$, $V=Y\cos\alpha-X\sin\alpha$ is uncorrelated iff $\tan2\alpha=\dfrac{2r\sigma_X\sigma_Y}{\sigma_X^2-\sigma_Y^2}$.
- $U=aX+bY$ and $V=cX+dY$ uncorrelated $\Rightarrow\sigma_U\sigma_V=\lvert ad-bc\rvert\,\sigma_X\sigma_Y\sqrt{1-r^2}$.
- Standardised $X,Y$: $r(aX+bY,\ bX+aY)=\dfrac{2ab+(a^2+b^2)r}{a^2+b^2+2abr}$.
- Spurious correlation of ratios $X_1/X_3$ and $X_2/X_3$ ($X_i$ uncorrelated): $\approx\dfrac{v_3^2}{\sqrt{(v_1^2+v_3^2)(v_2^2+v_3^2)}}$, $v_i$ the CVs.
-* Spearman: $\rho=1-\dfrac{6\sum d^2}{n(n^2-1)}$; add $\tfrac{m^3-m}{12}$ to $\sum d^2$ for each tie of size $m$; $\sum d^2=\tfrac{n(n^2-1)}3$ at $\rho=-1$. [2020 Q9] two individuals give only **−1 and 1**.
- Kendall $\tau=\dfrac{C-D}{n(n-1)/2}$. Concurrent deviations: $r_c=\pm\sqrt{\pm(2c-m)/m}$, $m=n-1$.
- Wrongly recorded data: correct the sums (or replace the wrong $d^2$ in $\sum d^2$) and recompute.
@grp Regression lines
$$y-\bar y=r\frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}(x-\bar x),\qquad x-\bar x=r\frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}(y-\bar y),\qquad r^2=b_{yx}b_{xy},\qquad \tan\theta=\frac{1-r^2}{\lvert r\rvert}\cdot\frac{\sigma_x\sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2}$$
- The two $b$’s share the sign of $r$; $\tfrac12(\lvert b_{yx}\rvert+\lvert b_{xy}\rvert)\ge\lvert r\rvert$.
- Unaffected by change of origin, not of scale: with $u=\tfrac{x-a}h$, $v=\tfrac{y-b}k$, $b_{yx}=\tfrac kh\,b_{vu}$.
- Perpendicular iff $r=0$; coincident iff $r=\pm1$.
-* Identification: call line 1 “$y$ on $x$” and line 2 “$x$ on $y$”; if $b_{yx}b_{xy}>1$, swap.
- $\min_a\mathrm{Var}(Y-aX)=(1-\rho^2)\,\mathrm{Var}\,Y$ ([2018 Q22] **(d)**); standard error of estimate $S_{yx}=\sigma_y\sqrt{1-r^2}$.
- Residuals: $\sum e=0$, $\sum xe=0$, $\sum\hat ye=0$. Regression from a joint pdf is $\mathbb{E}(Y\mid X=x)$, linear only in special cases.
.key **Worked pair.** $8x-10y+66=0$ and $40x-18y=214$ meet at $(13,17)$; $b_{yx}=0.8$, $b_{xy}=0.45$, $r=0.6$; with $\mathrm{Var}\,x=9$, $\sigma_y=4$. [2024 Q15]: “$b_{xy}=0.8$” is false, $r=0.6$ and $\hat y(30)=30.6$ are true: **II and III**. Classic pair $3x+2y=26$, $6x+y=31$: means $(4,7)$, $r=-0.5$.

@sec S5 | Curve fitting and orthogonal polynomials | Statistical Methods
@meta codes=S5 pyq=3 f27=0.5 tier=3
@lede Three PYQs: write the normal equations, choose the model with the smaller residual sum of squares, and know the orthogonal-polynomial values and the Hermite family.
@grp Curve fitting
- Line: $\sum y=na+b\sum x$ and $\sum xy=a\sum x+b\sum x^2$. Parabola adds $\sum x^2y=a\sum x^2+b\sum x^3+c\sum x^4$.
- Equally spaced $x$: code $u=(x-\text{middle})/h$ so that $\sum u=\sum u^3=0$; then $a=\bar y$ and $b=\sum uy/\sum u^2$.
-! Linearise: $y=ab^x$ and $y=ae^{bx}\to\log y$ on $x$; $y=ax^b\to\log y$ on $\log x$. This minimises errors in $\log y$, not in $y$.
-* Pick the model with the smaller residual SS: [2023 Q37] $13.36<15.48$, so $Y(6)=-36+48+2=$ **14**.
@grp Orthogonal polynomials
- $b_k=\sum y\xi_k'\big/\sum\xi_k'^2$; adding a higher degree leaves lower coefficients unchanged, and each degree adds its own $\mathrm{SS}=(\sum y\xi_k')^2\big/\sum\xi_k'^2$.
- Unit spacing, $u=x-\bar x$: $\xi_1=u$, $\xi_2=u^2-\tfrac{n^2-1}{12}$, $\xi_3=u^3-u\,\tfrac{3n^2-7}{20}$.
| $n$ | Linear | Quadratic | Cubic | Quartic |
| 3 | −1, 0, 1 | 1, −2, 1 | – | – |
| 4 | −3, −1, 1, 3 | 1, −1, −1, 1 | −1, 3, −3, 1 | – |
| 5 | −2, −1, 0, 1, 2 | 2, −1, −2, −1, 2 | −1, 2, 0, −2, 1 | 1, −4, 6, −4, 1 |
-* Hermite polynomials (orthogonal under the normal weight): $1,\ x,\ x^2-1,\ x^3-3x$ ([2021 Q17]), $x^4-6x^2+3$.
- [2024 Q11] pairs of quadratic forms are orthogonal when their coefficient vectors are: $(1,2,1)\cdot(1,2,1)=6$, $(1,-2,-1)\cdot(2,1,-1)=1$, $(1,2,-2)\cdot(2,1,2)=0$, $(1,1,-1)\cdot(1,1,-1)=3$, so **(c) three** pairs are non-orthogonal (pair III is built to give 0; a matrix inner product would make all four non-orthogonal).

@sec S6 | Bivariate normal | Statistical Methods
@meta codes=S6 pyq=25 f27=2.6 tier=1
@lede Read the BVN parameters off its exponent (a multi-question stem is common), then use linear regression with constant conditional variance. With S8 this is the biggest Statistical Methods block (58 PYQs, about 5.5 in 2027).
@grp Bivariate normal
$$f(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_1\sigma_2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{z_1^2-2\rho z_1z_2+z_2^2}{2(1-\rho^2)}\right\},\qquad z_i=\frac{x_i-\mu_i}{\sigma_i}$$
$$Y\mid X=x\ \sim\ N\left(\mu_2+\rho\frac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}(x-\mu_1),\ \sigma_2^2(1-\rho^2)\right)\quad\text{(linear and homoscedastic)}$$
-* Marginals are $N(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2)$ ([2025 Q18] **(a)**). Independent iff $\rho=0$ ([2018 Q27] **all three**; [2026 Q29] **III only**, since the means are free).
-! $(X,Y)$ is BVN iff every $aX+bY$ is normal ([2021 Q14] **1 only**); normal marginals alone are not enough.
- No density iff $\lvert\rho\rvert=1$, i.e. $\mathrm{Cov}^2=\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^2$: [2020 Q31] $\alpha=\pm\sqrt6$.
- MGF $\exp\left\{t_1\mu_1+t_2\mu_2+\tfrac12(\sigma_1^2t_1^2+2\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2t_1t_2+\sigma_2^2t_2^2)\right\}$. [2022 Q13] $\mathrm{Var}(X+Y)=300$, so $\mathbb{E}e^{X+Y}=$ **e^{180}**.
- $aX+bY\sim N(a\mu_1+b\mu_2,\ a^2\sigma_1^2+b^2\sigma_2^2+2ab\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2)$. [2024 Q12] $\mathrm{Var}(2X-3Y)=16+17-24=9$: $\tfrac1{\sqrt{18\pi}}e^{-z^2/18}$.
- Standard BVN$(0,0,1,1,\rho)$: $\mathbb{E}X^2Y^2=1+2\rho^2$ ([2022 Q15] **(d)**), $\mu_{31}=3\rho$, odd-order product moments vanish, $\mathrm{Cov}(X^2,Y^2)=2\rho^2$.
-* $X+Y\perp\!\!\!\perp X-Y$ iff $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$; $\mathrm{Var}(X\pm Y)=2(1\pm\rho)$ in standard form ([2022 Q18] **all three**).
- $Y-\rho X\perp\!\!\!\perp X$ with variance $1-\rho^2$: [2023 Q39] $\mathrm{Cov}=$ **0**; [2023 Q31] $\mathbb{E}[(Y-\rho X)^4\mid X]/(1-\rho^2)^2=$ **3**.
- $\tfrac{x^2-2\rho xy+y^2}{1-\rho^2}\sim\chi^2_2$, so $X^2+Y^2-2\rho XY$ has MGF **(1−2t+2ρ²t)^{−1}** [2023 Q40].
-* $P(X>0,Y>0)=\tfrac14+\tfrac{\sin^{-1}\rho}{2\pi}$; $P(XY<0)=\tfrac{\cos^{-1}\rho}{\pi}$, so $\rho=\cos(\pi q)$.
-* $\mathbb{E}\max(X,Y)=\sqrt{(1-\rho)/\pi}$ ([2021 Q15] **(d)**); for $\rho=0$ it is $1/\sqrt\pi$ ([2019 Q36]).
- $X/Y\sim\mathrm{Cauchy}\left(\rho,\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\right)$; $\rho(X,\Phi(Y))=\rho\sqrt{3/\pi}$; $\rho(X,\Phi(X))=\sqrt{3/\pi}$.
- [2019 Q7] $(X+\theta Z,\,Y+\theta Z)$ has $\rho=\tfrac{\theta^2}{1+\theta^2}=\tfrac14$, so $\lvert\theta\rvert=$ **1/√3**.
@grp Reading a BVN off its exponent (multi-question stems)
.key Write the exponent as $-(ax^2+bxy+cy^2+\text{linear terms})$. The precision matrix is $P=\begin{pmatrix}2a&b\\b&2c\end{pmatrix}$, $\Sigma=P^{-1}$, the mean solves $\nabla=0$, $2\pi f(\mu)=\sqrt{\lvert P\rvert}$, and $Y\mid x$ has mean $\mu_y-\tfrac{b}{2c}(x-\mu_x)$ and variance $\tfrac1{2c}$.
