@sec P9 | Characteristic, moment and probability generating functions | Probability
@meta codes=P9 pyq=21 f27=2.6 tier=1
@lede Generating functions are the fastest-rising block (7 PYQs in 2024, 5 in 2025): identify a law from its MGF, apply $M_{aX+b}$, use the compound rule $M_N(\ln M_X)$, and condition to get CFs of products (21 PYQs, about 2.6 in 2027).
@grp Core identities
- $M(t)=\mathbb{E}e^{tX}$, $\phi(t)=\mathbb{E}e^{itX}$, $G(s)=\mathbb{E}s^X$ (non-negative integers), $K(t)=\ln M(t)$.
-* $M_{aX+b}(t)=e^{bt}M(at)$ and $\phi_{aX+b}(t)=e^{ibt}\phi(at)$. Independent sums multiply generating functions and add cumulants.
- $M^{(r)}(0)=\mathbb{E}X^r$; $\phi^{(r)}(0)=i^r\mathbb{E}X^r$; $G'(1)=\mathbb{E}X$, $G''(1)=\mathbb{E}X(X-1)$, $\mathrm{Var}X=G''(1)+G'(1)-G'(1)^2$; $P(X=k)=G^{(k)}(0)/k!$.
- Cumulants: $\kappa_1=\mu$, $\kappa_2=\mu_2$, $\kappa_3=\mu_3$, $\kappa_4=\mu_4-3\mu_2^2$; $\kappa_r(aX+b)=a^r\kappa_r$ for $r\ge2$. [2025 Q12] all three statements, **(d)**.
- [2023 Q11] $Y=10-X$, $X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(10,\tfrac14)$: $\kappa_3(Y)=-npq(q-p)=$ **−15/16**.
- Poisson: every $\kappa=\lambda$. Normal: $\kappa_r=0$ for $r\ge3$, and a polynomial cumulant function forces normality. $\mathrm{Gamma}(n,1)$: $\kappa_r=n(r-1)!$, $\mu_r'=\Gamma(n+r)/\Gamma(n)$, $\phi=(1-it)^{-n}$.
- $\mu_r'=(r+1)!\,2^r$ is the MGF $(1-2t)^{-2}$ of $\chi^2_4$.
@grp CF statements (verdict fuel)
-* A CF always exists; $\phi(0)=1$, $\lvert\phi\rvert\le1$, $\phi$ is uniformly continuous, $\phi(-t)=\overline{\phi(t)}$. [2021 Q8] **1, 2 and 3**.
-! $\phi$ real $\iff$ $X$ symmetric about 0 (the Cauchy CF is real, yet there is no mean).
- Closure: $\lvert\phi\rvert^2$, $\mathrm{Re}\,\phi$, $\phi^n$, products, mixtures, $e^{\lambda(\phi-1)}$ and $p/(1-q\phi)$ are all CFs.
-! $\phi_{X+Y}=\phi_X\phi_Y$ does not certify independence: $X$ Cauchy and $Y=X$ satisfy it.
- $\lvert\phi(t_0)\rvert=1$ for some $t_0\ne0\Rightarrow X$ (after a shift) is lattice with span $2\pi/\lvert t_0\rvert$.
- $\phi(t)=1+o(t^2)$ near $0\Rightarrow X\equiv0$; so $e^{-t^4}$ and $e^{-\lvert t\rvert^3}$ are not CFs, while $e^{-\lvert t\rvert^\alpha}$ is a CF for $0<\alpha\le2$.
- Pólya: real, even, convex on $(0,\infty)$, decreasing, $\phi(0)=1\Rightarrow$ a CF. $(1-\lvert t\rvert)^+$ is the CF of $f(x)=\tfrac{1-\cos x}{\pi x^2}$.
-! $\phi$ differentiable at 0 does not guarantee $\mathbb{E}X$ exists; $\phi''(0)$ existing does force $\mathbb{E}X^2<\infty$.
- Inversion: if $\int\lvert\phi\rvert<\infty$, $f(x)=\tfrac1{2\pi}\int e^{-itx}\phi(t)\,dt$, bounded and continuous. Integer-valued $X$: $P(X=k)=\tfrac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-itk}\phi(t)\,dt$.
-* Uniqueness: $\phi$ determines $F$; agreement on an interval does not. Lévy continuity: $\phi_n\to\phi$ with $\phi$ continuous at $0\iff X_n\xrightarrow{d}X$; $N(0,n)$ gives a limit discontinuous at 0 and no limit law.
- An MGF finite near 0 gives all moments and fixes the law; all moments finite does not (lognormal).

@sec P10 | Modes of convergence | Probability
@meta codes=P10 pyq=5 f27=0.7 tier=3
@lede Convergence, inequalities, zero–one laws, LLN and CLT give about three questions a year, and every one is hypothesis-checking: name the mode, inequality or law, then test its exact conditions.
@grp The implication map
.fig conv
-* **Only three implications come free:** a.s. $\Rightarrow$ P, $r$th mean $\Rightarrow$ P, P $\Rightarrow$ d.
- a.s. and $r$th mean: **neither implies the other.** $L^r\Rightarrow L^s$ for $s<r$ (Lyapunov).
- The dashed arrows are the only partial converses: in probability $\Rightarrow$ some subsequence converges a.s.; in distribution $\Rightarrow$ in probability **only when the limit is a constant**.
- No other implication holds in general.
@grp Counterexamples that settle convergence MCQs
| Sequence | a.s. | In prob. | Mean square | In dist. |
| $X_n=c_n\mathbf 1(U<n^{-2})$, $U\sim U(0,1)$ | Yes ($\sum n^{-2}<\infty$) | Yes | Iff $c_n^2/n^2\to0$: $c_n=\sqrt n$ yes ([2020 Q32] **both**); $c_n=n$ no (**a.s. only**) | Yes |
| $X_n=n\mathbf 1(U<1/n)$ | Yes | Yes | No: $\mathbb{E}X_n^2=n$, and $\mathbb{E}X_n=1\not\to0$ | Yes |
| Independent $X_n\sim$ Bernoulli$(1/n)$ | No (Borel–Cantelli II) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Independent $X_n\sim$ Bernoulli$(1/n^2)$ | Yes (Borel–Cantelli I) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Sliding indicators sweeping $[0,1]$ | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| $X_n=-X$, $X\sim N(0,1)$, limit $X$ | No | No | No | Yes |
| $X_n\equiv1/n$, limit 0 | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes* |
-! *$F_n(0)=0\not\to F(0)=1$, but 0 is not a continuity point of the limit, so convergence in distribution still holds.
@grp Rules and PYQ keys
-! Convergence in distribution concerns laws only: $X_n\xrightarrow{d}X$ and $Y_n\xrightarrow{d}Y$ do not give $X_n+Y_n\xrightarrow{d}X+Y$.
-* Slutsky: $X_n\xrightarrow{d}X$ and $Y_n\xrightarrow{P}c$ give $X_n+Y_n\xrightarrow{d}X+c$, $X_nY_n\xrightarrow{d}cX$ and $X_n/Y_n\xrightarrow{d}X/c$ ($c\ne0$). The second limit must be a constant.
- Continuous mapping keeps each mode. Limits in probability are a.s. unique. Pólya: $F_n\to F$ with $F$ continuous is uniform in $x$. Scheffé: pointwise convergence of densities $\Rightarrow$ convergence in distribution.
-! a.s. convergence says nothing about moments: $\mathbb{E}X_n\to\mathbb{E}X$ needs domination or uniform integrability.
- [2022 Q45] $F_n$ uniform on $[-n,n]$ tends to $\tfrac12$ everywhere: **no limiting distribution**.
- [2025 Q49] shifted exponential $e^{-(x-\theta)}$, $x\ge\theta$: $\bar X\xrightarrow{P}$ **1+θ**.
- [2022 Q3] $X_i/i$ has mean $-\tfrac14$ and fixed variance, so $Y_n=n^{-\alpha}\sum X_i/i\xrightarrow{P}0$ iff **α>1**.
- [2021 Q25] $P(S_n\le x\sqrt n)\to\Phi(2)$ forces $\mu=0$ and $x/\sigma=2$, so $x=4$; then $P(S_n\le\sqrt n/4)\to$ **Φ(1/8)**.

@sec P11 | Zero–one laws | Probability
@meta codes=P11 pyq=3 f27=0.5 tier=3
@lede Borel–Cantelli and the two 0–1 laws: name the hypothesis (independence? summability?) and the answer follows.
@grp Zero–one laws
- $\limsup A_n=\bigcap_n\bigcup_{k\ge n}A_k=\{A_n\text{ i.o.}\}$; $\liminf A_n=\{A_n\text{ eventually always}\}$; $P(\liminf A_n)\le\liminf P(A_n)\le\limsup P(A_n)\le P(\limsup A_n)$.
-* Borel–Cantelli I: $\sum P(A_n)<\infty\Rightarrow P(A_n\text{ i.o.})=0$, **with no independence needed** ([2021 Q7] **0**).
-! Borel–Cantelli II: independent and $\sum P(A_n)=\infty\Rightarrow P(A_n\text{ i.o.})=1$. Independence is essential: $A_n=A$ with $P(A)=\tfrac12$ gives $\tfrac12$.
- Borel 0–1 law: for independent events $P(A_n\text{ i.o.})$ is 0 or 1 as $\sum P(A_n)$ converges or diverges.
-! Kolmogorov 0–1 law: a tail event of an independent sequence has probability 0 or 1, and the law does not say which. Tail: $\{\sum X_n\text{ converges}\}$, $\{\bar X_n\text{ converges}\}$, $\{\limsup X_n>c\}$. Not tail: $\{X_1>0\}$, $\{\sum X_n>0\}$, $\{S_n=0\text{ i.o.}\}$.
- [2021 Q21] iid with finite mean: $\sum P(\lvert X\rvert>n/2)<\infty$, so $P(\lvert X_n\rvert>n/2\text{ i.o.})=$ **0**.
- [2022 Q46] a fixed pattern such as SFSF in Bernoulli trials occurs infinitely often with probability **1** (disjoint blocks are independent).

@sec P12 | Chebyshev and Kolmogorov inequalities | Probability
@meta codes=P12 pyq=7 f27=0.7 tier=3
@lede Spot “at least” next to a mean and a variance: that is Chebyshev. Kolmogorov’s inequality is its maximal form.
@grp Inequalities
-* Markov, $X\ge0$: $P(X\ge a)\le\mathbb{E}X/a$, so $P(X\ge t\mu)\le1/t$ ([2020 Q29] **(b)**). Useless when $a\le\mathbb{E}X$.
-* Chebyshev: $P(\lvert X-\mu\rvert\ge k\sigma)\le1/k^2$, i.e. $P(\lvert X-\mu\rvert<k\sigma)\ge1-1/k^2$. At least 75% within $2\sigma$ and 88.9% within $3\sigma$ (normal: 95.45%, 99.73%).
-! Chebyshev is sharp: $\mu\pm k\sigma$ with probability $\tfrac1{2k^2}$ each and $\mu$ otherwise attains it.
- One-sided (Cantelli): $P(X-\mu\ge k\sigma)\le\tfrac1{1+k^2}$. Unimodal symmetric (Camp–Meidell): $P(\lvert X-\mu\rvert\ge k\sigma)\le\tfrac4{9k^2}$.
-* Kolmogorov: independent, mean 0, finite variances: $P\left(\max_{k\le n}\lvert S_k\rvert\ge\varepsilon\right)\le\mathrm{Var}(S_n)/\varepsilon^2$. Chebyshev is its $n=1$ case, and it drives the SLLN.
-* Sample size for $\lvert\bar X-\mu\rvert<\varepsilon$ with probability $\ge1-\alpha$: $n\ge\sigma^2/(\varepsilon^2\alpha)$.
- Also: Chernoff $P(X\ge a)\le e^{-ta}M(t)$; Hölder $\mathbb{E}\lvert XY\rvert\le(\mathbb{E}\lvert X\rvert^p)^{1/p}(\mathbb{E}\lvert Y\rvert^q)^{1/q}$; Minkowski; Jensen; Lyapunov.

@sec P13 | Laws of large numbers and central limit theorems | Probability
@meta codes=P13 pyq=7 f27=0.9 tier=3
@lede Every LLN/CLT item is hypothesis bookkeeping: iid? finite mean? finite variance? independence? standardised?
@grp Laws of large numbers
| Law | Hypotheses | Conclusion |
| Chebyshev WLLN | Independent (or uncorrelated), $\mathrm{Var}(S_n)/n^2\to0$ | $\bar X_n-\mathbb{E}\bar X_n\xrightarrow{P}0$ |
| Markov WLLN | $\mathrm{Var}(S_n)/n^2\to0$, any dependence; or independent with $\mathbb{E}\lvert X_i\rvert^{1+\delta}$ bounded | Same |
| Khinchin WLLN | iid with finite mean; **no variance needed** | $\bar X_n\xrightarrow{P}\mu$ |
| Bernoulli and Poisson LLN | Bernoulli trials, fixed $p$ or varying $p_i$ | Relative frequency $\xrightarrow{P}p$: consistency of $f/n$ ([2021 Q10] **LLN**) |
| Gnedenko (iff) | $Y_n=(S_n-\mathbb{E}S_n)/n$ | WLLN iff $\mathbb{E}\left[Y_n^2/(1+Y_n^2)\right]\to0$ |
| Feller (iid) | $n\,P(\lvert X\rvert>n)\to0$ | WLLN with centring $\mathbb{E}[X;\lvert X\rvert\le n]$ |
| Kolmogorov SLLN, iid | $\mathbb{E}\lvert X\rvert<\infty$ (necessary and sufficient) | $\bar X_n\xrightarrow{a.s.}\mu$ ([2022 Q41] **E(X_k)<∞**) |
| Kolmogorov SLLN, independent | $\sum\mathrm{Var}(X_n)/n^2<\infty$ | $(S_n-\mathbb{E}S_n)/n\xrightarrow{a.s.}0$ |
| Borel SLLN | Bernoulli trials | $f_n/n\xrightarrow{a.s.}p$ |
- $X_i=\pm i$ with probability $\tfrac12$: $\mathrm{Var}(S_n)\approx n^3/3$, so the WLLN fails. $X_i=\pm i^\alpha$: holds iff $\alpha<\tfrac12$.
- $X_k=\pm2^k$ with probability $2^{-(2k+1)}$ each, 0 otherwise: $\mathrm{Var}X_k=1$, so the WLLN holds.
- Jointly normal, variance 1, correlation $\rho$ only between neighbours: $\mathrm{Var}(S_n)=n+2(n-1)\rho$, so the WLLN holds.
-! iid Cauchy: $\bar X_n$ is Cauchy for every $n$, so no LLN and no CLT. iid $U(0,1)$: $\left(\prod X_i\right)^{1/n}\xrightarrow{P}e^{-1}$ (for $U(0,\theta)$: $\theta/e$).
@grp Central limit theorems
-* Lindeberg–Lévy: iid with $0<\sigma^2<\infty$ gives $\sqrt n(\bar X-\mu)/\sigma\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1)$. **Finite variance is essential.**
- De Moivre–Laplace: $(X-np)/\sqrt{npq}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1)$; $P(X\le k)\approx\Phi\left((k+0.5-np)/\sqrt{npq}\right)$.
-! Lyapunov: $\sum\mathbb{E}\lvert X_i-\mu_i\rvert^{2+\delta}/s_n^{2+\delta}\to0$ is sufficient, not necessary. Lindeberg–Feller is necessary and sufficient under uniform asymptotic negligibility.
- CF proof skeleton: $\left(1-\tfrac{t^2}{2n}+o(\tfrac1n)\right)^n\to e^{-t^2/2}$, finished by Lévy continuity. Berry–Esseen error $\le C\rho/(\sigma^3\sqrt n)$.
-! Only the standardised sum converges; $S_n$ itself does not, and $\bar X_n$ collapses to $\mu$.
- Delta method: $\sqrt n\left(g(\bar X)-g(\mu)\right)\xrightarrow{d}N\left(0,\sigma^2g'(\mu)^2\right)$. Variance-stabilising: $\sqrt X$ (Poisson), $\sin^{-1}\sqrt p$ (proportion), $\tanh^{-1}r$ (correlation).
