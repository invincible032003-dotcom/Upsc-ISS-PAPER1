@grp Events, counting and Bayes
- Inclusion–exclusion: $P\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^nA_i\right)=\sum P(A_i)-\sum_{i<j}P(A_iA_j)+\sum_{i<j<k}P(A_iA_jA_k)-\cdots+(-1)^{n+1}P(A_1\cdots A_n)$. Truncating after an odd (even) number of terms gives an upper (lower) bound: Bonferroni inequalities.
- De Morgan: $\left(\bigcup A_i\right)^c=\bigcap A_i^c$ and $\left(\bigcap A_i\right)^c=\bigcup A_i^c$. $P(A\triangle B)=P(A)+P(B)-2P(AB)$; $P(A\cap B^c)=P(A)-P(AB)$.
- Bayes in odds form: posterior odds $=$ prior odds $\times$ likelihood ratio. For a continuous parameter: $f(\theta\mid x)\propto f(x\mid\theta)f(\theta)$.
- Total probability with conditioning: $P(A\mid C)=\sum_iP(A\mid B_iC)P(B_i\mid C)$.
- Multiplication for two draws without replacement: $P(A_1A_2)=\tfrac{r}{r+b}\cdot\tfrac{r-1}{r+b-1}$. Probability of exactly $m$ successes in $n$ independent trials with probabilities $p_i$: coefficient of $s^m$ in $\prod(q_i+p_is)$.
- Occupancy: $n$ distinguishable balls into $N$ cells: $N^n$ outcomes; number of empty cells has mean $N(1-1/N)^n$. Pigeonhole: $n>N$ balls force a shared cell.
@grp Random variables and distribution functions
- Discrete: $F(x)=\sum_{t\le x}p(t)$; continuous: $f=F'$ almost everywhere and $F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^xf$. For $Y=g(X)$ with $g$ increasing: $F_Y(y)=F_X(g^{-1}(y))$; decreasing: $F_Y(y)=1-F_X(g^{-1}(y))$ (continuous $X$).
- Hazard rate $h(x)=\tfrac{f(x)}{1-F(x)}$; survival $S=1-F=\exp\left(-\int_0^xh\right)$. Constant hazard $\iff$ exponential; increasing hazard: Weibull with shape $>1$.
- Raw and central moments: $\mu_r'=\mathbb{E}X^r$, $\mu_r=\mathbb{E}(X-\mu)^r$; $\mu_2=\mu_2'-\mu^2$; $\beta_1=\mu_3^2/\mu_2^3$, $\beta_2=\mu_4/\mu_2^2$. Factorial moments $\mu_{(r)}=\mathbb{E}X^{(r)}$; $\mu_2'=\mu_{(2)}+\mu_{(1)}$.
- Cumulants: $K(t)=\ln M(t)=\sum\kappa_r\tfrac{t^r}{r!}$; $\kappa_1=\mu$, $\kappa_2=\sigma^2$, $\kappa_3=\mu_3$, $\kappa_4=\mu_4-3\sigma^4$; $\kappa_r$ add over independent sums.
- Quantile $\xi_p$: $F(\xi_p)=p$; median $\xi_{1/2}$; quartiles $Q_1,Q_3$; mode maximises $f$. Skewness sign: right skew $\Rightarrow$ mean $>$ median.
- Standardisation: $Z=(X-\mu)/\sigma$ has mean 0, variance 1; $\mathbb{E}Z^3=\gamma_1$, $\mathbb{E}Z^4=\beta_2$.
@grp Random vectors: joint laws and the multivariate normal
- Joint pmf/pdf $f(x,y)\ge0$, $\sum\!\sum f=1$ or $\iint f=1$. Marginals by summing/integrating out; $f(y\mid x)=f(x,y)/f_X(x)$; $F(x,y)=\int_{-\infty}^x\int_{-\infty}^yf$, $f=\tfrac{\partial^2F}{\partial x\partial y}$.
- $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=\mathbb{E}XY-\mathbb{E}X\mathbb{E}Y$; $\mathrm{Var}(\sum a_iX_i)=\sum a_i^2\mathrm{Var}X_i+2\sum_{i<j}a_ia_j\mathrm{Cov}(X_i,X_j)$; $\mathrm{Cov}(aX+bY,cX+dY)=ac\,\mathrm{Var}X+bd\,\mathrm{Var}Y+(ad+bc)\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)$.
- Independence tests: $f=f_Xf_Y$; $F=F_XF_Y$; the joint MGF factorises $M(t_1,t_2)=M_X(t_1)M_Y(t_2)$.
- Multivariate normal $N_p(\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma)$: pdf $(2\pi)^{-p/2}\lvert\Sigma\rvert^{-1/2}\exp\left\{-\tfrac12(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)'\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)\right\}$; MGF $\exp\{\mathbf t'\boldsymbol\mu+\tfrac12\mathbf t'\Sigma\mathbf t\}$; $A\mathbf X\sim N(A\boldsymbol\mu,A\Sigma A')$; sub-vectors are normal; components are independent iff uncorrelated.
- Conditional normal: $\mathbf X_1\mid\mathbf X_2=\mathbf x_2\sim N\left(\boldsymbol\mu_1+\Sigma_{12}\Sigma_{22}^{-1}(\mathbf x_2-\boldsymbol\mu_2),\ \Sigma_{11}-\Sigma_{12}\Sigma_{22}^{-1}\Sigma_{21}\right)$; $(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)'\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf X-\boldsymbol\mu)\sim\chi^2_p$.
- Partial correlations from the inverse covariance matrix: $\rho_{ij\cdot\text{rest}}=-\dfrac{(\Sigma^{-1})_{ij}}{\sqrt{(\Sigma^{-1})_{ii}(\Sigma^{-1})_{jj}}}$.
@grp Expectation and conditional expectation
- $\mathbb{E}g(X,Y)=\iint g\,f$. Discrete: $\mathbb{E}(X\mid Y=y)=\sum xp(x\mid y)$; continuous: $\int xf(x\mid y)\,dx$.
- $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbb{E}(X\mid Z),\mathbb{E}(Y\mid Z))+\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y\mid Z)$.
- Wald’s identity: for a stopping time $N$ with $\mathbb{E}N<\infty$, $\mathbb{E}S_N=\mathbb{E}N\,\mathbb{E}X_1$.
- Prediction: the mean minimises $\mathbb{E}(X-c)^2$; the median minimises $\mathbb{E}\lvert X-c\rvert$; $\mathbb{E}(X\mid Y)$ minimises $\mathbb{E}(X-g(Y))^2$ over all $g$.
@grp Generating functions: how they relate
| Function | Definition | Gives | Existence |
| PGF | $G(s)=\mathbb{E}s^X$ | $p_k=G^{(k)}(0)/k!$; $G^{(r)}(1)=\mathbb{E}X^{(r)}$ | Non-negative integer $X$; $\lvert s\rvert\le1$ |
| MGF | $M(t)=\mathbb{E}e^{tX}$ | $M^{(r)}(0)=\mathbb{E}X^r$; $M(t)=G(e^t)$ | Only if finite near 0 |
| CF | $\varphi(t)=\mathbb{E}e^{itX}$ | $\varphi^{(r)}(0)=i^r\mathbb{E}X^r$; $\varphi(t)=M(it)=G(e^{it})$ | Always |
| Cumulant GF | $K(t)=\ln M(t)$ | $\kappa_r=K^{(r)}(0)$ | Where $M$ exists |
- Joint: $M(t_1,t_2)=\mathbb{E}e^{t_1X+t_2Y}$; $\mathbb{E}X^rY^s=\left.\partial_{t_1}^r\partial_{t_2}^sM\right\rvert_0$. Uniqueness: equal MGFs on a neighbourhood of 0 (or equal CFs) imply equal laws.
- Linear transformation: $M_{aX+b}(t)=e^{bt}M_X(at)$; sums of independent variables multiply MGFs, CFs and PGFs; $M_{\bar X}(t)=M_X(t/n)^n$.

@grp Precise statements
- **Borel–Cantelli lemmas.** I: $\sum P(A_n)<\infty\Rightarrow P(A_n\text{ i.o.})=0$. II: $A_n$ independent and $\sum P(A_n)=\infty\Rightarrow P(A_n\text{ i.o.})=1$.
- **Kolmogorov three-series theorem.** For independent $X_n$, $\sum X_n$ converges a.s. iff for some $c>0$: $\sum P(\lvert X_n\rvert>c)<\infty$, $\sum\mathbb{E}X_n\mathbf 1(\lvert X_n\rvert\le c)$ converges and $\sum\mathrm{Var}(X_n\mathbf 1(\lvert X_n\rvert\le c))<\infty$.
- **Lindeberg condition.** With $s_n^2=\sum\sigma_i^2$: $\dfrac1{s_n^2}\sum_i\mathbb{E}\left[(X_i-\mu_i)^2\mathbf 1(\lvert X_i-\mu_i\rvert>\varepsilon s_n)\right]\to0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. It implies the CLT and uniform asymptotic negligibility; conversely (Feller) CLT plus negligibility implies it.
- **Lévy continuity theorem.** $X_n\xrightarrow{d}X\iff\varphi_n(t)\to\varphi(t)$ for all $t$; if $\varphi_n\to g$ pointwise and $g$ is continuous at 0, then $g$ is a CF of some $X$ and $X_n\xrightarrow{d}X$.
- **Chebyshev WLLN** (independent, $\mathrm{Var}(S_n)/n^2\to0$); **Khinchin** (iid, finite mean); **Kolmogorov SLLN** (iid iff finite mean); **Etemadi**: pairwise independent identically distributed with finite mean also obey the SLLN.
- **Glivenko–Cantelli.** For iid $X_i$ with CDF $F$: $\sup_x\lvert F_n(x)-F(x)\rvert\to0$ a.s.
- **Lyapunov inequality** $(\mathbb{E}\lvert X\rvert^s)^{1/s}\le(\mathbb{E}\lvert X\rvert^r)^{1/r}$ for $0<s<r$; **Hölder** $\mathbb{E}\lvert XY\rvert\le\lVert X\rVert_p\lVert Y\rVert_q$, $\tfrac1p+\tfrac1q=1$; **Minkowski** $\lVert X+Y\rVert_p\le\lVert X\rVert_p+\lVert Y\rVert_p$.
- **Continuity correction.** $P(X\le k)\approx\Phi\left(\tfrac{k+0.5-\mu}{\sigma}\right)$ for integer-valued $X$; $P(X\ge k)\approx1-\Phi\left(\tfrac{k-0.5-\mu}{\sigma}\right)$.
- **Central limit for sums.** $P(S_n\le x)\approx\Phi\left(\tfrac{x-n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt n}\right)$; for the sample mean $P(\bar X\le x)\approx\Phi\left(\tfrac{\sqrt n(x-\mu)}{\sigma}\right)$.

@grp Data, location and dispersion
- Relative frequency $f_i/N$; cumulative frequency; frequency density $f_i/h_i$. Pie angle $=360^\circ\times f_i/N$; bar heights $\propto$ values.
- Grouped mean by step deviation: $\bar x=A+h\dfrac{\sum fu}{N}$, $u=\tfrac{x-A}{h}$. Combined mean of $k$ groups $\bar x=\dfrac{\sum n_i\bar x_i}{\sum n_i}$.
- Charlier’s check: $\sum f(u+1)^2=\sum fu^2+2\sum fu+N$.
- Median of grouped data: $L+\dfrac{N/2-c}{f}h$; quartiles $Q_i=L+\dfrac{iN/4-c}{f}h$; mode $L+\dfrac{f_1-f_0}{2f_1-f_0-f_2}h$.
- Variance: $\sigma^2=\dfrac{\sum fx^2}{N}-\bar x^2=h^2\left[\dfrac{\sum fu^2}{N}-\left(\dfrac{\sum fu}{N}\right)^2\right]$. Gini index $G=1-\sum(p_i-p_{i-1})(q_i+q_{i-1})$ from a Lorenz curve.
- Relations between averages: $\mathrm{AM}\ge\mathrm{GM}\ge\mathrm{HM}$; $\mathrm{GM}=\sqrt{\mathrm{AM}\cdot\mathrm{HM}}$ for two positive numbers; the GM of ratios is the ratio of GMs.
@grp Moments, skewness and kurtosis
- Raw to central: $\mu_2=\mu_2'-\mu_1'^2$; $\mu_3=\mu_3'-3\mu_2'\mu_1'+2\mu_1'^3$; $\mu_4=\mu_4'-4\mu_3'\mu_1'+6\mu_2'\mu_1'^2-3\mu_1'^4$. Central to raw: $\mu_2'=\mu_2+\mu_1'^2$, $\mu_3'=\mu_3+3\mu_2\mu_1'+\mu_1'^3$.
- Change of origin and scale: $\mu_r(y)=b^r\mu_r(x)$ for $y=a+bx$; $\beta_1,\beta_2$ are unchanged; $\gamma_1$ changes sign if $b<0$.
- Coefficient of skewness: Karl Pearson $\dfrac{\text{Mean}-\text{Mode}}{\sigma}$; Bowley $\dfrac{Q_3+Q_1-2M}{Q_3-Q_1}$; moment $\gamma_1=\dfrac{\mu_3}{\sigma^3}$. Kurtosis $\beta_2=\dfrac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2}$; $\beta_2\ge1$; $\beta_2\ge\beta_1+1$.
@grp Association and contingency
- Goodman–Kruskal $\gamma=\dfrac{C-D}{C+D}$ for ordinal tables; Kendall $\tau_b=\dfrac{C-D}{\sqrt{(C+D+T_x)(C+D+T_y)}}$.
- Class-frequency identities: $(A)=(AB)+(A\beta)$; $(AB)=(ABC)+(AB\gamma)$; $N=(A)+(\alpha)$.
- Coefficient of mean square contingency $\phi^2=\chi^2/N$; for a $2\times2$ table $\phi^2=\dfrac{(ad-bc)^2}{(a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d)}$.
@grp Curve fitting and orthogonal polynomials
- Orthogonal polynomials for $n$ equally spaced $x$ with $u=x-\bar x$ and unit spacing: $\xi_1=u$; $\xi_2=u^2-\tfrac{n^2-1}{12}$; $\xi_3=u^3-\tfrac{3n^2-7}{20}u$; $\xi_4=u^4-\tfrac{3n^2-13}{14}u^2+\tfrac{3(n^2-1)(n^2-9)}{560}$. Recurrence $\xi_{r+1}=u\,\xi_r-\dfrac{r^2(n^2-r^2)}{4(4r^2-1)}\xi_{r-1}$.
- Fit $y=\sum_kb_k\xi_k$ with $b_k=\dfrac{\sum y\xi_k}{\sum\xi_k^2}$; regression sum of squares of degree $k$ is $b_k\sum y\xi_k$; residual $\mathrm{SS}=\sum y^2-\sum_kb_k\sum y\xi_k$.
- Exponential $y=ae^{bx}$: fit $\ln y=\ln a+bx$. Power $y=ax^b$: fit $\ln y=\ln a+b\ln x$. Modified exponential and logistic use three-point or partial-sum methods.
- Second-degree normal equations: $\sum y=na+b\sum x+c\sum x^2$; $\sum xy=a\sum x+b\sum x^2+c\sum x^3$; $\sum x^2y=a\sum x^2+b\sum x^3+c\sum x^4$.
@grp Regression and correlation
- Regression of $y$ on $x$: $b_{yx}=\dfrac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}}$, $a=\bar y-b\bar x$; $\mathrm{SE}(b)=\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{S_{xx}}}$, $s^2=\dfrac{S_{yy}-bS_{xy}}{n-2}$; $t=b/\mathrm{SE}(b)\sim t_{n-2}$. Coefficient of determination $R^2=\dfrac{b\,S_{xy}}{S_{yy}}=r^2$.
- ANOVA identity $S_{yy}=\text{SSR}+\text{SSE}$; $F=\dfrac{\text{SSR}/1}{\text{SSE}/(n-2)}=t^2$.
- Confidence interval for $\rho$: $\tanh\left(z\pm\dfrac{1.96}{\sqrt{n-3}}\right)$ with $z=\tanh^{-1}r$. Testing $\rho=\rho_0$: $\dfrac{z-\tanh^{-1}\rho_0}{1/\sqrt{n-3}}\sim N(0,1)$.
- Correlation ratio decomposition: $S_{yy}=\text{between}+\text{within}$; $\eta^2=\dfrac{\text{between}}{S_{yy}}$; intraclass $r=\dfrac{\mathrm{MSB}-\mathrm{MSW}}{\mathrm{MSB}+(k-1)\mathrm{MSW}}$.
- Multiple correlation from a correlation matrix $R$: $R_{1.23\ldots}^2=1-\dfrac{\lvert R\rvert}{R_{11}}$; multiple regression normal equations $\hat{\mathbf b}=S^{-1}\mathbf s$.
@grp Standard errors, intervals and tests
- Confidence intervals (level $1-\alpha$): mean, $\sigma$ known $\bar x\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt n$; $\sigma$ unknown $\bar x\pm t_{n-1,\alpha/2}s/\sqrt n$; difference of means $\bar x_1-\bar x_2\pm t\,S_p\sqrt{1/n_1+1/n_2}$; proportion $\hat p\pm z\sqrt{\hat p\hat q/n}$; variance $\left(\dfrac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{n-1,\alpha/2}},\ \dfrac{(n-1)s^2}{\chi^2_{n-1,1-\alpha/2}}\right)$.
- Test statistics: $z=\dfrac{\bar x-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt n}$; $t=\dfrac{\bar x-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt n}$; two proportions $z=\dfrac{\hat p_1-\hat p_2}{\sqrt{\hat P\hat Q(1/n_1+1/n_2)}}$; variance ratio $F=S_1^2/S_2^2$.
- Power of the one-sided $z$ test for $H_1:\mu=\mu_1>\mu_0$: $1-\Phi\left(z_\alpha-\dfrac{(\mu_1-\mu_0)\sqrt n}{\sigma}\right)$.
- Neyman–Pearson: the most powerful test rejects when the likelihood ratio $\dfrac{L(\theta_1)}{L(\theta_0)}>k$; the size is $\alpha=P(\text{reject}\mid H_0)$.
- Paired data: $t=\dfrac{\bar d}{s_d/\sqrt n}$, $n-1$ df. Large-sample test for $\rho=0$: $z=r\sqrt{n}$ approximately (for large $n$).
@grp Non-parametric tests and order statistics
- Sign test with normal approximation: $z=\dfrac{S-n/2\pm0.5}{\sqrt n/2}$; exact $S\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\tfrac12)$. Wilcoxon signed-rank with ties: $\mathrm{Var}\,T^+=\tfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{24}-\tfrac1{48}\sum(t_i^3-t_i)$.
- Two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov: $D=\sup_x\lvert F_m(x)-G_n(x)\rvert$; large-sample 5% cut-off $1.36\sqrt{\dfrac{m+n}{mn}}$.
- Runs test large-sample: $z=\dfrac{R-\mathbb{E}R}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}R}}$. Kruskal–Wallis ties: divide $H$ by $1-\dfrac{\sum(t^3-t)}{N^3-N}$.
- $\chi^2$ goodness of fit: $\sum\dfrac{(O-E)^2}{E}\sim\chi^2_{k-1-m}$ with $m$ estimated parameters; independence in an $r\times c$ table $(r-1)(c-1)$ df.
- Range of $n$ iid observations: $f_R(r)=n(n-1)\int[F(x+r)-F(x)]^{n-2}f(x)f(x+r)\,dx$. Expected maximum of $n$ iid $N(0,1)$: $0.564$ ($n=2$), $0.846$ ($n=3$), $1.029$ ($n=4$), $1.163$ ($n=5$), $1.539$ ($n=10$).
- Sample quantile: $\xi_{\lceil np\rceil:n}$ is asymptotically $N\left(\xi_p,\dfrac{p(1-p)}{nf(\xi_p)^2}\right)$.
