- Binomial skew is positive if $p<\tfrac12$, zero if $p=\tfrac12$, negative if $p>\tfrac12$. $\mu_4=3(npq)^2+npq(1-6pq)$ for the binomial and $3\lambda^2+\lambda$ for the Poisson.
@grp Shortcuts
-* Binomial from mean $a$ and variance $b$: $q=b/a$, $n=a^2/(a-b)$, which must be a positive integer. **Variance $\ge$ mean is impossible** (mean 6, variance 8 dies at once).
- Recurrences: binomial $\tfrac{P(x+1)}{P(x)}=\tfrac{n-x}{x+1}\cdot\tfrac pq$; Poisson $P(x+1)=\tfrac{\lambda}{x+1}P(x)$. Poisson $P(k)=P(k+1)$ gives $\lambda=k+1$.
-* $P(X\text{ even})=\tfrac12\{1+G(-1)\}$: binomial $\tfrac12\{1+(q-p)^n\}$, Poisson $\tfrac12\{1+e^{-2\lambda}\}>\tfrac12$.
- $n-X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)$. **Binomials add only with a common $p$;** Poissons always add.
- $\mathbb{E}\left[\tfrac1{X+1}\right]=\tfrac{1-q^{n+1}}{(n+1)p}$ for $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$ and $\tfrac{1-e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}$ for Poisson$(\lambda)$.
- Poisson approximation: $\lambda=np$, good for large $n$ and small $p$.
-* Poisson split: $X\mid X+Y=n\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(n,\tfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}\right)$. Thinning Poisson$(\lambda)$ with $p$ gives Poisson$(p\lambda)$, and the kept and dropped streams are independent.
-! $X-Y$ and $2X$ are not Poisson. Raikov: $X\perp\!\!\!\perp Y$ with $X+Y$ Poisson forces both to be Poisson.
- Independent Poissons: $P(X=Y)=e^{-(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)}\sum_r\tfrac{(\lambda_1\lambda_2)^r}{(r!)^2}$; $P(X-Y=r)=$ coefficient of $t^r$ in $\exp(\lambda_1t+\lambda_2/t-\lambda_1-\lambda_2)$.
- Independent $\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)$: $P(X=Y)=\sum_r\left[\binom nrp^rq^{n-r}\right]^2$. A coin tossed $2n$ times: $P(\text{heads}>\text{tails})=\tfrac12\left[1-\binom{2n}{n}4^{-n}\right]$.
- Geometric: the minimum of independent Geo$(p_1)$, Geo$(p_2)$ is Geo$(p_1+p_2-p_1p_2)$; the minimum of $k$ iid copies is Geo$(1-q^k)$. For iid geometrics, $\min(X,Y)\perp\!\!\!\perp X-Y$.
- Multinomial: $X_i\mid X_j=m\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(n-m,\tfrac{p_i}{1-p_j}\right)$ and $\rho_{ij}=-\sqrt{\tfrac{p_ip_j}{(1-p_i)(1-p_j)}}$.
-* Count triage: binomial variance $<$ mean, Poisson $=$, negative binomial $>$.
@grp Links between families
- Binomial–Beta: $P(X\ge k)=P\left(\mathrm{Beta}(k,n-k+1)\le p\right)$.
- Binomial–$F$: $P(X\le k-1)=P\left(F_{2k,\,2(n-k+1)}>\tfrac{n-k+1}{k}\cdot\tfrac pq\right)$.
- Binomial–negative binomial: $F_X(r-1)=1-F_Y(n-r)$, where $Y$ = failures before the $r$-th success.
- Poisson–$\chi^2$: $P(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\le k)=P(\chi^2_{2(k+1)}>2\lambda)$.
-* Limits: hypergeometric $\to$ binomial $\to$ Poisson ($np\to\lambda$); negative binomial $\to$ Poisson ($rq\to\lambda$); binomial and Poisson $\to$ normal.

@grp Normal: Φ-algebra and reading the exponent
- Coverage 68.27%, 95.45%, 99.73% at $1,2,3\sigma$. Two-tailed $z$: 1.645 (10%), 1.96 (5%), 2.58 (1%); one-tailed: 1.28, 1.645, 2.33.

-* Complete the square: $f\propto\exp(-x^2/8+2x)=\exp\{-(x-8)^2/8+8\}$ gives $\mu=$ **8**, $\sigma^2=$ **4** [2019 Q38].

- [2019 Q35] 1000 round-off errors $U(-0.05,0.05)$: $\mathrm{SD}(S)=\sqrt{1000\times0.01/12}=0.913$, so $P(\lvert S\rvert<2)=2\Phi(2.19)-1=$ **0.97**.

@grp Exponential and gamma
-* $\max_\theta P(a\le X\le2a)$: with $u=e^{-a\theta}$, $u-u^2$ peaks at $\tfrac14$ [2020 Q34].
- Variance $>$ mean iff $\theta<1$ (rate form): [2018 Q10] **all three statements**; [2023 Q3] **1 only** (memorylessness holds for every $\theta$; $X_{(1)}$ is exponential, not $\chi^2$).
-* Minimum of independent $\mathrm{Exp}(\theta_i)$ is $\mathrm{Exp}(\sum\theta_i)$; $P(X_1<X_2)=\tfrac{\theta_1}{\theta_1+\theta_2}$. [2018 Q13] rates 1 and 2: **1/3**.
- iid exponentials: $X-Y$ is Laplace [2019 Q40]; $\lvert X-Y\rvert\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)$, so [2018 Q12] $P(\lvert X-Y\rvert\le10)=$ **1−e^{−10}**.
- Sum of $n$ iid $\mathrm{Exp}(\theta)$ is $\mathrm{Gamma}(n,\theta)$; their mean is $\mathrm{Gamma}(n,n\theta)$ [2023 Q12] **(d)**. $2\theta X\sim\chi^2_2$.
- Given $X_1+X_2=t$ (iid exponential), $X_1\sim U(0,t)$. $\lfloor X\rfloor$ is geometric with $p=1-e^{-\theta}$.
- Gamma ratios: $\tfrac X{X+Y}\sim$ Beta-I$(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$, independent of $X+Y$ [2018 Q9]; $\tfrac XY\sim$ Beta-II$(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$.
- $X\sim$ Beta-I$(u,v)$ independent of $Y\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(\lambda,u+v)$ gives $XY\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(\lambda,u)$. $\mathbb{E}\sqrt{\mathrm{Gamma}(m,1)}=\Gamma(m+\tfrac12)/\Gamma(m)$.

@grp Characterisation cards
-* Memoryless: geometric (discrete) and exponential (continuous) only [2020 Q25].
- Maximum entropy: uniform on a bounded interval; exponential on $(0,\infty)$ with fixed mean; normal with fixed variance.
- $\bar X\perp\!\!\!\perp S^2$ only for the normal (Geary). $X+Y\perp\!\!\!\perp X-Y$ for iid $X,Y$ only for the normal (Bernstein). $X\perp\!\!\!\perp Y$ with $X+Y$ normal forces both normal (Cramér).
- MGF exists everywhere: normal, binomial, Poisson, uniform. Near 0 only: exponential, gamma, Laplace, geometric, negative binomial. Useless: Cauchy, lognormal ($t>0$), Pareto, $t$. The CF always exists.
